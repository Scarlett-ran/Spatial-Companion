# Exploration Phase 04 — Next-Phase Decisions

The following are experience and user-validation objectives that the project needs to address over time; they are not all covered by the current browser demo. The Browser stage tests only the legibility of interaction rules and causality while gathering early directional signals for U1 and U2. D2 requires on-device interaction and remains the first experience objective after the project moves onto the target device.

## O1 — Can a Subjective Sense of Contact Form Without Physical Haptic Feedback?

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Assumption | D2: Visual and behavioral feedback can create a credible subjective sense of contact in the absence of physical haptic feedback. |
| Evidence to observe | Whether participants spontaneously describe “touching” Baobao and attribute the response to their own hand movement; when they experience their hand passing through, latency, or merely triggering an animation; whether the presence, absence, or timing of feedback changes the experience. |
| Lowest-cost method | On-device, implement only one contact region on the forehead and one response action. Compare conditions with and without a synchronized response, and record the approach, contact, and hand-withdrawal sequence, followed by neutral questions about the experience. Without a device, review only the feedback timing; do not treat video viewing or mouse interaction as validation of touch. |
| Claim boundary | Results can support only a subjective sense of contact under specific conditions. They do not represent physical haptics and cannot independently prove companionship value. Tracking and latency issues must be documented separately from experiential judgments. |

## O2 — Does a Spatial Pet Without Long-Term Care Obligations Have Value?

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Assumption | U1: Even without feeding, check-ins, punishment, or long-term care obligations, quiet coexistence and lightweight responses can still provide companionship value. |
| Evidence to observe | How participants distinguish companionship from decoration; the specific contexts in which they would keep or close Baobao; whether they understand that ignoring or leaving the pet causes no loss; whether the no-care design reduces burden or instead weakens the sense of connection. |
| Lowest-cost method | If user research is conducted, begin with a small number of concept interviews using the current demo and a scenario card that explicitly states the four no-care rules. Use neutral questions to record reasons for use, reasons for rejection, and participants’ understanding of the burden involved. If firsthand experience needs to be examined, then have the minimum prototype accompany a short task, allowing participants to ignore or close it at any time. |
| Claim boundary | Concept interviews provide only leads about expected value and understanding of the rules. A short-term experience also cannot prove long-term companionship effects, value after the novelty wears off, or improvements to mental health. |

## O3 — Are Young Users Willing to Accept This Concept?

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Assumption | U2: Gen Z and younger users are willing to accept a spatial pet and the device conditions it requires. |
| Evidence to observe | Participants’ real-world barriers to pet ownership, alternatives they currently use, specific reasons to try or reject the concept, and how device availability and wearing conditions affect their choice; distinguish liking Baobao from willingness to use the product. |
| Lowest-cost method | If user research is conducted, O3 can be combined with O2 in a small set of interviews, but U2 should be documented separately. Ask first about participants’ actual life contexts and alternative behaviors, then present the concept and explain the device conditions. If research is deferred, compile only the questions to ask and draw no conclusions about acceptance. |
| Claim boundary | A small number of interviews provides only directional signals. Stated interest is not equivalent to adoption, purchase, or sustained use, and cannot represent an entire generation of young users. |
