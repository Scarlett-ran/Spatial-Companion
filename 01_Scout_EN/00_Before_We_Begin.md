# Before We Begin

I think of Baobao as a companion or friend, rather than a pet in the traditional sense.

A pet often implies a relationship built around care: the animal relies on you for food and attention, and much of its daily life revolves around its owner. I want to create a small animal with a world of his own. He is independent and brave, with his own life, friends, and journeys. He enjoys playing with you and may choose to spend time beside you, because the two of you have become friends rather than because he cannot live without you.

The idea began with the Beijing swift. Every spring, these birds travel from southern Africa to Beijing to nest and breed. In late summer they set out again for southern Africa, with the young birds leaving before the adults.

Swifts are not pets. They fly, feed, and survive in a world of their own, facing the weather, distance, and uncertainty of migration. Yet people still care about these animals whose lives barely intersect with theirs. They look for the birds overhead, share sightings, rescue injured swifts, and wish the young birds a safe journey. The swifts do not change their lives for us, but for a moment their journey and ours share the same point of view.

Emotional connection does not have to depend on ownership or caregiving. A life can remain free and independent while its courage, vulnerability, persistence, and travels still move us.

That is the relationship I want Baobao to express. He does not depend on the user for food and does not punish them for being away. He has his own state and rhythm. He can rest, observe his surroundings, and respond when the user chooses to approach. The user and Baobao are two beings with lives of their own who occasionally meet and gradually become friends.

> **Baobao has a life of his own. Sometimes, that life intersects with yours.**

He keeps you company because you are friends, not because he needs you to survive.
