# Before We Begin

I prefer to think of Baobao as a companion or a friend, rather than a pet in the traditional sense.

A pet usually suggests a relationship built around care: the animal depends on you for food and attention, and its daily life revolves around its owner. But I want to create a small animal with a world of his own. He is independent and brave, with his own life, friends, and journeys. He enjoys playing with you and is happy to come and spend time beside you, not because he cannot live without you, but because you happen to have become good friends.

This idea was first inspired by the Beijing swift. Every spring, these birds travel a great distance from southern Africa to Beijing to nest and breed. In late summer, they set off again to spend the winter in southern Africa. The first to begin this long migration are the young swifts, born only a short time before; the adults leave a little later.

Swifts are, of course, not human pets. They have their own world. They fly, feed, and survive independently, facing the weather, distance, and uncertainty of a long migration. Yet people are moved by these small animals whose lives have almost no direct connection to their own, calling them “friends from Beijing.” During migration season, people share their swift sightings online and follow where the birds have travelled. Some volunteer to rescue injured swifts and help them return to the sky. Others say, “Everyone, pause work and wish the first young swifts a safe journey to Africa.” People celebrate them, worry about them, and sometimes cannot help crying when they hear that the birds have arrived safely.

I believe an emotional connection between two living beings does not have to be built on ownership or care. Even when a life remains free in a world of its own, its courage, vulnerability, perseverance, and distant journeys can still matter deeply to us. There are many ways to form a connection with the world.

That is why the spatial companion I hope to create will not depend on users to feed him or punish them for not paying attention to him for a while. He has his own state and rhythm of life. He can rest quietly, observe his surroundings, and respond to your gaze and touch when you choose to approach. You are less like an owner and a pet, and more like two beings with lives of their own who happen to meet and gradually become friends.

> **Baobao has a life of his own. Sometimes, that life intersects with yours.**

He keeps you company not because he needs you to survive.

Simply because you are friends.
