# Baobao — Validation and Next Steps

[Scout index](README.md) · [中文](../01_Scout_CN/04-validation-and-next-steps.md) · [Project index](../README.md)

**Current status:** FWP 0.1.0 is available as a locally runnable build and a static deployment package. Public deployment and user trials have not yet been completed. This document records the agreed sequence, boundaries, delivered scope, and conditions for moving forward. Trial arrangements remain to be determined.

## 1. Learning goals and work sequence

**Build the FWP → user trials and refinement → build a prototype on glasses → spatial experience trials.**

Explore how users understand Baobao's life and interactions, what they gain from spending time together, what feels dull or burdensome, and what should change next. Users may come for fun, companionship, relaxation, or comfort; they do not need to share a single motivation.

The Functional Web Prototype (FWP) is a functional web experience intended to be accessible through its own link and support repeated use. “Prototype” on its own refers to the later minimum feasible prototype on glasses. The FWP cannot answer questions about the value of sharing a real space or the burden of wearing glasses.

## 2. FWP delivery status and remaining work

Build one version following [Experience and Prototype](03-experience-and-prototype.md): a simplified room, mouse or touch input, three life states, one consistent toy, the agreed interactions, and continuity over time. Do not build a separate comparison version for proactive approaches.

Before delivery, check that core behaviors work, movements and states transition naturally, repeated input and page interruptions recover correctly, and the experience is accessible independently. Self-testing checks implementation; it is not evidence of user value.

## 3. Inviting users and gathering feedback

Invite people who are open to virtual companions and would like companionship and enjoyment at home. They can interact, watch quietly, or leave. They do not have to endorse the relationship premise in advance, like real animals, or own XR equipment.

Keep feedback lightweight and concrete: what happened, which actions were unclear, which moments they liked or disliked, what they wanted to see on reopening, and whether leaving or not responding felt burdensome. Listen to specific experiences before explaining the design. Separate quotations, observations, and researcher interpretations, and note failures or prompts. Distinguish arranged sessions from spontaneous returns. Stated willingness to use is not actual use, and a lack of input does not establish an emotional state.

**To be determined:** participant count, session length, study period, feedback channels, and event recording. Arrange these around the completed FWP's actual scope rather than inheriting parameters from the old study. If the old concept video is used, keep reactions to it separate from feedback on FWP use.

## 4. Deciding what to do next

| What happens | Next step |
|---|---|
| The core experience works, users have tried it, and major points of confusion have been addressed | Proceed to the prototype on glasses. |
| Movements, states, controls, or time continuity are unclear | Identify and address the main confusion. Distinguish actual failures from unclear behavioral expression; do not use a story to explain away a problem. |
| Users generally find the experience dull or burdensome | Understand why, then decide what to change or whether to continue. Do not immediately add tasks, relationship upkeep, or a content system. |
| Feedback is mixed, there has been too little opportunity for use, or users have not yet been invited | Keep the uncertainty explicit. Technical completion does not establish experiential value, and missing feedback does not by itself count as failure. |

Whether repeated time together gradually produces an emotional bond remains an ongoing question. A small trial cannot establish a lasting relationship. Preserve specific examples, counterexamples, limitations, and the changes made in each feedback synthesis; do not reduce them to intimacy or interaction scores.

## 5. Moving to glasses and spatial trials

Once the device and required capabilities are clear, build an indoor prototype with a floor area for movement and one placement surface. Set up and test the area in advance; do not include movement between rooms. No device has been selected. Lightweight AI glasses, a concept video, or a web presentation do not establish spatial capabilities. Assess missing models, animation, and development work against actual conditions.

First check whether presentation, positioning, occlusion, interaction, and entry and exit are ready for trials. Then explore what “Baobao and I live in the same real space” means to users, and whether wearing and operating the glasses gets in the way.

If the spatial experience adds no clear value, first check whether presentation and interaction are adequate. If it still adds no value, reconsider the medium or product direction rather than automatically turning Baobao into a screen-based pet. If the device or resources are not ready, state what is needed to proceed; do not keep expanding the FWP simply to fill the wait.
